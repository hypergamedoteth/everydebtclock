    How it works

    Official annual figures · projection labelled · sources linked

    Why this number doesn't tick. Earlier official years are in the history table, but the 2023→2024 change (−0.5%) is within about two standard errors of the Census estimate, so there is no defensible trend to project. The figure shown is the official total.

    How often it's published. Annual. The Census Bureau's Annual Survey of State and Local Government Finances comes out about two years after the fiscal year (survey year 2024: July 2026; survey year 2023: July 2025). The next Census figures, for survey year 2025, are expected around mid-2027, based on the last two release dates.

    Per Missourian divides by the Census Bureau's estimate of 6,270,541 residents on Jul 1, 2025. It is not projected forward, so the live per-Missourian figure rises only with the debt.

    Note. Census publishes Missouri's state + local total as $34.28 billion for 2024 (coefficient of variation 0.70%) and $34.47 billion for 2023. State + local here is exactly Census's state figure plus its local figure.

    Note. Census's figures are the newest official state + local totals that use one definition for all 50 states, but they lag about two years, so the page shows the official figure as of June 30, 2024. Missouri's own audited report is fresher but is not used here: it covers a narrower scope and would not be comparable with the other states.

    Note. The 2023→2024 change (−$0.19 billion) is smaller than about two standard errors of the Census estimate (about $0.48 billion at a 0.7% coefficient of variation), so Census's data does not show which way the debt is moving.

    Note. This page therefore shows the official figure as published and does not tick it forward.

    Note. Large swings in Census's series for Missouri: 2021→2022: −$13.31 billion (-27%). Census's tables give no explanation for these moves and we have not found one, so read the history table with care: they may be real payoffs or issuance, or a change in how Census measured or classified debt. Census's 2022 local-government figures come from the full Census of Governments rather than the sample used in other years, so a shift around 2022 may partly reflect that change of method.

    Note. Not included: unfunded pension and retiree-health liabilities, and the state's share of the federal debt.

    Note. Missouri's state fiscal year ends June 30. Census survey year 2024 covers each government's fiscal year that ended between July 1, 2023 and June 30, 2024; the clock dates it June 30, 2024, the end of that window.

    Note. Figures from source tables may not add exactly because of rounding in the source.
