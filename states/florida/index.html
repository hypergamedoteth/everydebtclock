    Florida State & Local Debt Clock

    Florida state and local governments owed $132,570,328,000 ($132.57 billion) at the end of fiscal year 2024 (Jun 30, 2024), according to the US Census Bureau, Annual Survey of State and Local Government Finances, table GS00LOCALFIN: $20.14 billion of state government debt and $112.43 billion of local government debt. That is $5,650.30 per Floridian: the debt divided by the Census Bureau's estimate of 23,462,518 Florida residents on Jul 1, 2025. The Census Bureau reports Florida's state + local debt once a year, not daily. This page shows the official figure for Jun 30, 2024 and does not tick it forward, because the 2023→2024 change (+1.1%) is within about two standard errors of the Census estimate. Earlier official years are in the What changed table.

    How it works

    Official annual figures · projection labelled · sources linked

    Why this number doesn't tick. Earlier official years are in the history table, but the 2023→2024 change (+1.1%) is within about two standard errors of the Census estimate, so there is no defensible trend to project. The figure shown is the official total.

    How often it's published. Annual. The Census Bureau's Annual Survey of State and Local Government Finances comes out about two years after the fiscal year (survey year 2024: July 2026; survey year 2023: July 2025). The next Census figures, for survey year 2025, are expected around mid-2027, based on the last two release dates. The state's own report (shown as a secondary line) updates sooner.

    Per Floridian divides by the Census Bureau's estimate of 23,462,518 residents on Jul 1, 2025. It is not projected forward, so the live per-Floridian figure rises only with the debt.

    Note. Census publishes Florida's state + local total as $132.57 billion for 2024 (coefficient of variation 1.96%) and $131.12 billion for 2023. State + local here is exactly Census's state figure plus its local figure.

    Note. Census's figures are the newest official state + local totals that use one definition for all 50 states, but they lag about two years, so the page shows the official figure as of June 30, 2024. The state's own report (Florida Division of Bond Finance, 2025 Debt Report) is fresher but narrower; it is shown separately and never added. Census does not publish a reconciliation to state reports, so we do not itemize the gap.

    Note. The 2023→2024 change (+$1.45 billion) is smaller than about two standard errors of the Census estimate (about $5.20 billion at a 1.96% coefficient of variation), so Census's data does not show which way the debt is moving.

    Note. This page therefore shows the official figure as published and does not tick it forward.

    Note. About the state's own figure: Not included: local government debt; indirect debt (about $12.6 billion); university direct-support-organization debt; unfunded pension and retiree-health liabilities; and Florida's share of the federal debt.

    Note. Not included: unfunded pension and retiree-health liabilities, and the state's share of the federal debt.

    Note. Florida's fiscal year ends June 30. Census survey year 2024 covers each government's fiscal year that ended between July 1, 2023 and June 30, 2024; the clock dates it June 30, 2024, the end of that window.

    Note. Figures from source tables may not add exactly because of rounding in the source.
