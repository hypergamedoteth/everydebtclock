    US State Debt Clocks

    Every place with a debt clock, built from figures its own government publishes. A clock ticks only where the official trend is reliable; otherwise the official figure is shown as published. Each clock shows who publishes the numbers and how often, with every number linked to its source. Most of these governments report their debt once a year or once a month, so the ticking numbers are labelled projections from the latest official figures. Each tile says what it counts: state clocks count state and local government debt (cities, counties, school and special districts included), and countries are shown in their own currency. The World Debt Clock adds one stock per economy in US dollars, using the best source available.

    Questions

    What do the US state debt clocks count?

    Each clock counts Census Bureau debt outstanding for one state or the District of Columbia. For the 50 states that is state government debt plus local government debt. The District is published as one government total, because Census has no separate state level for it. The latest Census date on these pages is Jun 30, 2024.

    Where do the per-resident and debt-to-GDP figures come from?

    Debt per resident divides that Census total by the Census Bureau population estimate, Jul 1, 2025. Debt-to-GDP divides the same Census total by Bureau of Economic Analysis current-dollar GDP for the BEA calendar year named on each state page. The two dates are both named, because the debt year and the population year are not the same release.

    Which states have a debt clock?

    All 50 states and the District of Columbia are listed. 25 clocks extend the latest official change forward by the second. 26 hold the official total, because that change is not a rate this site will extend. The reason is on the state page.
