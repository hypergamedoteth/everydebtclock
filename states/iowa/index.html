That is $7,825.97 per Iowan: the debt divided by the Census Bureau's estimate of 3,238,387 Iowa residents on Jul 1, 2025. How it works

Official annual figures · projection labelled · sources linked

The live number = latest official total + (last year's official change per second × seconds since that total). The Census Bureau publishes Iowa's state + local debt once a year, not daily, so this is a projection between annual reports, not an official figure.

projected = 25,343,524,000 + 24.0223 × (seconds since Jun 30, 2024 11:59:59 PM CT)

The rate is each part's official change over its latest year, added up: state debt $8,055,307,000 − $7,745,293,000 = $310,014,000; local debt $17,288,217,000 − $16,838,587,000 = $449,630,000; combined $759,644,000 over 366 days = $24.02 per second. The actual path is lumpy (bonds are sold in batches and repaid on schedules), so the projection will drift; when new annual reports come out, the clock re-bases to the new official figures.

How often it's published. Annual. The Census Bureau's Annual Survey of State and Local Government Finances comes out about two years after the fiscal year (survey year 2024: July 2026; survey year 2023: July 2025). The next Census figures, for survey year 2025, are expected around mid-2027, based on the last two release dates.

Per Iowan divides by the Census Bureau's estimate of 3,238,387 residents on Jul 1, 2025. It is not projected forward, so the live per-Iowan figure rises only with the debt.

Note. Census publishes Iowa's state + local total as $25.34 billion for 2024 (coefficient of variation 0.99%) and $24.58 billion for 2023. State + local here is exactly Census's state figure plus its local figure.

Note. Census's figures are the newest official state + local totals that use one definition for all 50 states, but they lag about two years, so the live clock projects from June 30, 2024. Iowa's own audited report is fresher but is not used here: it covers a narrower scope and would not be comparable with the other states.

Note. The 2023→2024 change (+3.1%) is large enough to measure and small enough to extend, so the live number continues it as a straight line from June 30, 2024. Treat it as a projection, not a reported figure.

Note. Not included: unfunded pension and retiree-health liabilities, and the state's share of the federal debt.

Note. Iowa's state fiscal year ends June 30. Census survey year 2024 covers each government's fiscal year that ended between July 1, 2023 and June 30, 2024; the clock dates it June 30, 2024, the end of that window.

Note. Figures from source tables may not add exactly because of rounding in the source.
