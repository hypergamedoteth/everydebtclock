California Debt Clock

California state and local governments owed $597,201,490,000 ($597.20 billion) at the end of fiscal year 2024 (Jun 30, 2024), according to the US Census Bureau, Annual Survey of State and Local Government Finances, table GS00LOCALFIN: $166.39 billion of state government debt and $430.81 billion of local government debt. That is $15,174.61 per Californian: the debt divided by the Census Bureau's estimate of 39,355,309 California residents on Jul 1, 2025. The clock below starts from that official figure and projects forward at last year's pace (about $44.1M a day), the official change from Jun 30, 2023 to Jun 30, 2024. The Census Bureau reports California's state + local debt once a year, not daily, so the ticking number is a projection, not an official figure; it re-bases when the next annual reports come out.

How it works

Official annual figures · projection labelled · sources linked

The live number = latest official total + (last year's official change per second × seconds since that total). The Census Bureau publishes California's state + local debt once a year, not daily, so this is a projection between annual reports, not an official figure.

projected = 597,201,490,000 + 510.9217 × (seconds since Jun 30, 2024 11:59:59 PM CT)

The rate is each part's official change over its latest year, added up: state debt $166,390,802,000 − $162,000,792,000 = $4,390,010,000; local debt $430,810,688,000 − $419,044,129,000 = $11,766,559,000; combined $16,156,569,000 over 366 days = $510.92 per second. The actual path is lumpy (bonds are sold in batches and repaid on schedules), so the projection will drift; when new annual reports come out, the clock re-bases to the new official figures.

How often it's published. Annual. The Census Bureau's Annual Survey of State and Local Government Finances comes out about two years after the fiscal year (survey year 2024: July 2026; survey year 2023: July 2025). The next Census figures, for survey year 2025, are expected around mid-2027, based on the last two release dates. The state's own report (shown as a secondary line) updates sooner.

Per Californian divides by the Census Bureau's estimate of 39,355,309 residents on Jul 1, 2025. It is not projected forward, so the live per-Californian figure rises only with the debt.

Note. Census publishes California's state + local total as $597.20 billion for 2024 (coefficient of variation 0.52%) and $581.04 billion for 2023. State + local here is exactly Census's state figure plus its local figure.

Note. Census's figures are the newest official state + local totals that use one definition for all 50 states, but they lag about two years, so the live clock projects from June 30, 2024. The state's own report (California State Controller, audited ACFR FY2025) is fresher but narrower; it is shown separately and never added. Census does not publish a reconciliation to state reports, so we do not itemize the gap.

Note. About the state's own figure: Figures are principal outstanding from Table 13. The ACFR's Debt Administration summary reports $112.6 billion of bonded debt, which includes unamortized bond premiums; we leave premiums out so the figure counts what the State has to repay at maturity.

Note. About the state's own figure: The State Treasurer's 2025 Debt Affordability Report counts $81.67 billion of General Fund-supported and self-liquidating GO and lease revenue bonds at June 30, 2025; it leaves out agency revenue bonds that the ACFR includes.

Note. About the state's own figure: Not included: local government debt; the University of California (reported as a discretely presented component unit); commercial paper and other short-term borrowings; conduit debt; leases; unfunded pension and retiree-health liabilities; and California's share of the federal debt.

Note. Not included: unfunded pension and retiree-health liabilities, and the state's share of the federal debt.

Note. California's fiscal year ends June 30. Census survey year 2024 covers each government's fiscal year that ended between July 1, 2023 and June 30, 2024; the clock dates it June 30, 2024, the end of that window.

Note. Figures from source tables may not add exactly because of rounding in the source.
