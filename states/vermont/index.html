How it works

Official annual figures · projection labelled · sources linked

Why this number doesn't tick. Earlier official years are in the history table, but the 2023→2024 change (+5.9%) is too large to carry forward as a trend, so there is no defensible trend to project. The figure shown is the official total.

How often it's published. Annual. The Census Bureau's Annual Survey of State and Local Government Finances comes out about two years after the fiscal year (survey year 2024: July 2026; survey year 2023: July 2025). The next Census figures, for survey year 2025, are expected around mid-2027, based on the last two release dates.

Per Vermonter divides by the Census Bureau's estimate of 644,663 residents on Jul 1, 2025. It is not projected forward, so the live per-Vermonter figure rises only with the debt.

Note. Census publishes Vermont's state + local total as $4.15 billion for 2024 (coefficient of variation 0.89%) and $3.92 billion for 2023. State + local here is exactly Census's state figure plus its local figure.

Note. Census's figures are the newest official state + local totals that use one definition for all 50 states, but they lag about two years, so the page shows the official figure as of June 30, 2024. Vermont's own audited report is fresher but is not used here: it covers a narrower scope and would not be comparable with the other states.

Note. The 2023→2024 change (+$0.23 billion, +5.9%) is more than 5% of the 2023 level in one year. A move that size is more likely a one-off (a large bond issue or payoff, a reclassification, or Census sampling) than a trend, and a straight line carried a further two years past it would drift far from any plausible figure.

Note. This page therefore shows the official figure as published and does not tick it forward.

Note. Not included: unfunded pension and retiree-health liabilities, and the state's share of the federal debt.

Note. Vermont's state fiscal year ends June 30. Census survey year 2024 covers each government's fiscal year that ended between July 1, 2023 and June 30, 2024; the clock dates it June 30, 2024, the end of that window.

Note. Figures from source tables may not add exactly because of rounding in the source.
