National debt under William Howard Taft

Answer. The Treasury total was $2,626,806,272 on Jul 1, 1908 and $2,868,373,874 on Jul 1, 1912 (the end of the last term). The difference is $241,567,602 (+9.2%), so the debt was higher. That is the change between those two Treasury figures. It is not a measure of what the president added: Congress writes tax and spending laws, and terms are not the same length. Per person: $29.61 per person at the first record (Census July 1, 1908), and $30.09 per person at the last record (Census July 1, 1912).

From Apr 1, 1993 on, a figure is the last Debt to the Penny close on or before the date. Earlier, it is the last Historical Debt Outstanding record on or before the date, an annual total, not the debt on inauguration day. A dash means those two dates fall on the same Treasury record, so there is nothing to subtract.

Per person divides the Treasury figure by the Census Bureau's July 1 resident population of that calendar year. It is not the population on the exact day. This site's July 1 series starts in 1900, and it has no July 1 figure for a later year until Census publishes it, so those cells are blank. Source for the population: Census Bureau population estimates, the same files as the national per-person page.

Questions

What was the national debt under William Howard Taft?

The Treasury total was $2,626,806,272 on Jul 1, 1908 and $2,868,373,874 on Jul 1, 1912 (the end of the last term). The difference is $241,567,602 (+9.2%), so the debt was higher. That is the change between those two Treasury figures. It is not a measure of what the president added: Congress writes tax and spending laws, and terms are not the same length.

Does the change show what the president added to the debt?

No. It is the difference between the Treasury total at the start and at the end. Term lengths differ, and spending and tax laws are passed by Congress.

Is the per-person figure the population on that day?

No. It is the Treasury figure divided by the Census Bureau's July 1 resident population of that calendar year, when that year is in the series this site uses. The series starts in 1900.
